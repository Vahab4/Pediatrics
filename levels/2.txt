PSYCHOMOTOR DEVELOPMENT OF A CHILD: INNATE UNCONDITIONED REFLEXES OF NEWBORNS
1. NEUROLOGICAL EXAMINATION ALGORITHM & DIAGNOSTIC METHODS
Algorithm for Infant Neurological Examination:
1. Visual inspection
2. Assessment of motor activity levels and establishment of motor skills
3. Evaluation of muscle tone
4. Evaluation of the reflex sphere (tendon reflexes, segmental automatisms, and suprasegmental postural automatisms)
Classification of Motor Automatisms:
• Segmental Reflexes: Palmar mandibular reflex (Babkin), search reflex, grasping reflex (Robinson), Moro reflex, defense reflex, Galant reflex, Perez reflex, support reflex, and crawling reflex.
• Suprasegmental Reflexes: Air-righting reflex (symmetrical and asymmetrical), upper Landau reflex, lower Landau reflex, and neck- and self-righting reflex.
Diagnostic & Research Methods:
1. Electroencephalography (EEG)
2. Doppler ultrasonography of cerebral vessels
3. Neurosonography, MRI, and CT imaging of the brain
4. Global and spicular electromyography (EMG) and electroneuromyography (ENMG)
5. Polysomnography
6. Lumbar puncture
Main Pediatric Neurological Syndromes:
• Hyperexcitability syndrome
• Depression (hypoexcitability) syndrome
• Intracranial hypertension syndrome
• Convulsive states
• Syndrome of motor disorders
• Syndrome of vegetative-visceral disorders
• Meningeal syndrome and meningism
2. ORAL & FEEDING REFLEXES
Search Reflex (Kussmaul Search Reflex):
• Elicitation: Gently stroke the skin near the corner of the mouth without touching the lips (best performed before feeding while the infant is calm).
• Response: The infant turns the head toward the stimulus, lowers the bottom lip, and protrudes the tongue to search for the breast.
• Timeline: Normally present up to 3–4 months of age.
Proboscis Extension Reflex:
• Elicitation: Lightly tap a finger on the infant's upper lip.
• Response: The orbicularis oris contracts, pouting/folding the lips forward into a proboscis.
• Timeline & Pathology: Present in all healthy newborns and fades by 3 months of age. Persistence beyond 3 months suggests underlying central nervous system pathology.
Sucking Reflex:
• Elicitation: Place the infant supine and insert a nipple or finger into the mouth.
• Response: Rhythmic sucking movements occur immediately.
Palmar Mandibular Reflex (Babkin Reflex):
• Elicitation: Apply gentle pressure with the thumbs to both of the infant's palms.
• Response: The infant opens the mouth and slightly flexes the head forward.
• Timeline: Present from birth to 2 months, after which it rapidly diminishes and disappears.
3. SPINAL & POSTURAL REFLEXES
Palmar Grasp Reflex (Robinson Reflex):
• Elicitation: Place a finger into the infant's open palm.
• Response: The infant firmly grasps the finger; the grip is often strong enough to lift the infant slightly off the surface.
• Timeline: Weakens by 3–4 months and is replaced by voluntary, conscious grasping.
Perez Reflex:
• Elicitation: Run a finger firmly from the coccyx upward along the midline of the back over the spinous processes (always performed last during an exam due to infant discomfort).
• Response: The infant arches the trunk, flexes the limbs, cries, and frequently urinates.
• Timeline: Disappears by approximately 3 months of age.
Galant Reflex:
• Elicitation: Stroke the paravertebral skin from bottom to top, roughly 1.5–2 cm lateral to the spinous processes.
• Response: The infant curves the trunk laterally (concavity) toward the stimulated side.
• Timeline: Persists up to 3–4 months, then gradually fades.
Positive Support and Automatic Walking Reflex:
• Elicitation: Hold the infant upright under the axillae (supporting the head) and place the soles of the feet on a flat surface.
• Response: In Phase I, the infant flexes the legs; upon feeling the surface, Phase II occurs as the infant extends the legs to "stand." Tilting the infant slightly forward triggers stepping movements (automatic walking).
• Timeline: Fades by 1–1.5 months of age. Voluntary, purposeful standing and walking emerge around 1 year of age.
Defense Reflex:
• Elicitation: Place the newborn prone (on the stomach).
• Response: The infant automatically turns the head to the side—a vital protective mechanism that keeps the airway unobstructed for breathing.
Crawling Reflex (Bauer Reflex):
• Elicitation: Place the infant prone and apply gentle palm support against the soles of the feet.
• Response: The infant pushes off the support and makes coordinated crawling movements (can also occur spontaneously without plantar support, posing a fall risk on changing tables).
• Timeline: Persists up to 4 months of age before fading.
Moro Reflex (Startle Reflex):
• Elicitation: Triggered by a sudden stimulus, such as clapping hands, striking the exam surface 10–15 cm away from the infant, abruptly extending the legs, or suddenly lowering the infant's position in space.
• Response: Occurs in two phases—Phase I involves abduction and extension of the arms with spreading of the fingers, followed by Phase II, where the arms adduct forward in an embracing or "covering" motion.
• Timeline: Weakens and disappears by 4 months of age.
